# LockIn Glossary

Terms used across PRD, CLAUDE.md, Roadmap, and competitive docs that have specific meanings inside this project. When the same term shows up in two documents, it should mean the same thing here.

## Wedge vs Durable Moat

These are related but **not interchangeable**. Both have been used loosely across LockIn docs; this is the canonical distinction.

**Wedge.** The narrow, specific entry product that gets users in the door. For LockIn, the wedge is the P1 product: mood-and-energy-aware scheduling that captures longitudinal behavioral data from day one. The wedge is what users experience and adopt first; it is also the capture infrastructure that builds the moat over time.

**Durable Moat.** The structural defensibility that keeps competitors out once the user base is established. For LockIn, the moat is the longitudinal behavioral dataset itself — mood, energy, completion patterns, context over weeks and months — which cannot be bought, scraped, or replicated by a general-purpose agent without equivalent capture infrastructure.

The wedge produces the moat. They are causally linked, not synonymous: a product can have a wedge and never develop a moat (commodity feature, no compounding data), and a moat without a wedge is a dataset nobody is paying for.

## Usage rules

- Use **wedge** when discussing P1 product strategy, market entry, or "what users adopt first."
- Use **durable moat** (or just **moat**) when discussing long-term defensibility, what compounds with use, or what blocks competitors.
- Do not say "the wedge is the moat" — they are different abstractions over the same underlying phenomenon (longitudinal behavioral data), one read as product, one read as defensibility.
